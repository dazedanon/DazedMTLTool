Cross-cutting rules for this corpus. Each one is here because the source does
it constantly, not because it might.

**「」 around a whole line is the engine's speech convention, not a quotation.**
Nearly every line in this game is `\NAME[speaker]「...」`. Render the line as
plain English dialogue with no quotation marks. Use quotation marks only where
a speaker is quoting someone else inside their own line.

**A line that opens with （ and closes with ） is inner monologue.** Keep the
parentheses. It is Eris thinking, and the game uses the difference between what
she thinks and what she says as its main device.

**A leading full-width space is the author's continuation indent.** It marks
"this is the second line of the same speech" and carries no meaning in English -
the tool strips it before you see it and re-wraps the result. Never reproduce
it, and never write your own line breaks: the box is re-wrapped afterwards.

**An interior full-width space is a pacing beat**, roughly a comma's worth of
silence. Render it as a space, or as a comma or dash where English needs the
punctuation. Do not run the two phrases together.

**Trailing ……, ーー and ！？ are the author's punctuation.** Keep them as
`...`, `--` and `!?`. A line that is nothing but them is a beat of silence and
stays one.

**Moans, gasps and onomatopoeia become English sounds.** No romaji, no kana:

    あぁ / んっ / はぁ        Aah... / Ngh... / Haah...
    イく / イっちゃう          I'm cumming / I'm gonna cum
    ぴちゃ ぐちゅ じゅぽ      wet sounds - slurp, squelch, schlick
    くぱぁ                     the spreading-open sound; describe it
    ンンッ、ふぅ、あぐっ      keep the rhythm and the count of beats

A drawn-out vowel (`あああああ`) stays drawn out (`Aaaaah`). A stutter
(`だ、だめ`) stays a stutter (`N-no`). Length and repetition are performance
directions, so match them rather than tidying them up.

**♡ and ❤ are punctuation.** They mark arousal in the voice and belong exactly
where the source has them, including at the end of a word (`yes♡`). Never drop
them, never add them.

**〇 and ● are censorship, and they stay.** `ちん〇` is `c〇ck`, not `cock`. A
name written `●●●` stays `●●●`.

**Sound-effect words written in katakana in narration** (`ドンッ`, `ズブリ`)
become English sound words in the same position, not a description of the
sound.

**The dummy name in a battle-log fragment is scaffolding.** Fragments like
`は倒れた！` are drawn straight after a character's name. Write the whole
sentence including the dummy name you were given; it is stripped afterwards.

**Numbers are load-bearing.** `三日後` is "three days later". A price, a count
or a level in the source appears unchanged in the output.
