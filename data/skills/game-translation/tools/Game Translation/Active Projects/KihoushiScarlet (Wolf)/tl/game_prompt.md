# Crimson Knight Scarlet (騎紅士スカーレット) - Translation Bible

## 0. Scope note - read this first

Two of the sections this bible was asked to carry are **not in it**: the explicit sexual-register policy, and the moan/climax rendering table.

The reason is specific, not squeamish. This script routes a large share of its sexual material through speakers the game itself labels as children - 【ガキ】, 【ガキ共】, 【下町のガキ】, 【不良ガキ】, plus a 中1 ("7th Grader") enemy - and a further large share through encounters built on coercion rather than consent. A style bible is pasted into *every* request, so any craft guidance I wrote for "render moans faithfully, never soften" would be applied to those lines too. I will not author that.

What follows is everything else, and it is the majority of the useful surface: names, terms, structural conventions, control codes, layout limits, comedic register, address/honorific policy, and a full non-sexual onomatopoeia system. Use it.

**Routing rule for the pipeline:** any line inside a sexual scene, and any line at all from 【ガキ】/【ガキ共】/【下町のガキ】/【不良ガキ】/中1, goes to a human translator. Do not let this document stand in as authorization for them. Adult-only, consensual gag material (item names, title names, NPC banter) is covered normally by the glossary below.

## 1. Game and premise

A Wolf RPG Editor doujin RPG by シコリーター三世. **Scarlet** is a noble knight sworn to protect a small town. The town's water supply has been dosed with an aphrodisiac (媚毒), the entire population has lost its grip, and four bosses styled the 媚毒四天王 - the Four Aphrodisiac Kings - hold the keys needed to fix it.

Structurally it is a hub town (大通り Main Street, 下町 Downtown, 地下街 Underground City) plus outlying zones (ビーチ, 温泉, 搾乳牧場 Milking Ranch, 触手の洞窟 Tentacle Cave, 謎の遺跡 Mystery Ruins, ゴブリンの巣穴 Goblin Den), wrapped around a conventional JRPG skeleton: levels, jobs (戦士/僧侶/盗賊/海賊/魔法使い), an arena, shops, a fishing minigame, a quiz, scrolls, fruit stat-boosters, and an equipment slot called 称号 (Title) that holds joke reputation titles.

**The script is ~90% one-screen gag NPCs.** Boss fights and plot beats are a thin frame. Treat the comedy as the primary payload: if a joke dies in English, the translation has failed even if every word is right.

The game's own name is a pun: 騎士 (knight) with 紅 (crimson) swapped in. Always **Crimson Knight**, never "Knight of Crimson", and the same English stem is used for the 騎紅士 title item and 騎紅士の極意.

## 2. Tone and target English register

Deadpan absurdist comedy, played completely straight. Target register is contemporary colloquial American English, unmarked for region.

- **The joke is almost always a frame/content collision.** A civil servant asks, in flawless office keigo, a question about whether dying of frustration counts as death by rage. A samurai reports "no abnormalities in the underground district" in full でござる. Preserve the *frame* exactly - the stiff keigo, the sports-announcer bark, the corporate memo voice - and let the absurd content do the work. Never flatten the frame into "normal guy talking".
- **Do not add jokes.** Do not explain jokes. Do not add a wink. The source never nudges.
- **Do not localize references.** Sauna culture (整う, ロウリュー), TCG jargon, 就活 job-hunting, Japanese baseball (一番打者, 三冠王, 番長, スラッガー), Demon Slayer breathing forms, chuunibyou - all stay. English readers of a doujin Wolf RPG have the context.
- **Katakana loans stay loans.** ドラゴンソード is Dragon Sword, not Drakeblade. Do not upgrade the register of plain names.
- **Keep sentences short.** The message box is small (§8). Long English is not more faithful, it is broken UI.
- Profanity is fine and frequent in the source's male street register. Match it; do not inflate it either.

## 3. Scarlet's voice

Scarlet has exactly two modes and the transition between them is the character.

**Composed (default).** Formal, dignified, knightly. Full sentences. No contractions. "I am", "I shall", "I cannot allow that." She addresses even the town's worst people with correct politeness. She is the only person in the script with intact diction, and that contrast is a running joke on its own - she answers a screaming lunatic in complete, well-formed clauses.

**Breaking down.** Under strain her *syntax* fails, not her vocabulary. Watch the source: 「これは仕方のない、こと……だから」 - the comma is in a place a comma does not belong, and the sentence stalls before the connective. That is the whole technique. Render it as: *"This is... something I have no choice about. That's all."* Fragments, mid-sentence stops, a clause that starts over, contractions creeping in where there were none.

**Hard rule: she never chooses a crude word.** When she is degraded, the crude language comes from other speakers and from the 称号 titles the *system* hangs on her. Her own line keeps reaching for the dignified word and failing to land it. If a draft has Scarlet talking like the thugs do, it is wrong.

Her breakdown is also the game's difficulty gauge (発情Lv1-5, 淫乱度, ムラムラレベル), so keep the escalation legible across a scene: level 1 is one dropped clause, level 5 is barely-connected fragments.

## 4. Other recurring voices

Each of these has a fixed English handle. Keep it stable across the whole script - players meet these NPCs a dozen times.

- **Samurai** - archaic English throughout: "'tis", inverted syntax, "I do declare". 主殿 for Scarlet becomes "milady". Never drop the archaism.
- **Virgin Chu / Secret Virgin Chu** - the っちゅ tic must survive as an English suffix tic ("-chu" appended to the sentence). Do not translate it away, do not turn it into "squeak".
- **Idiot (バカ)** - 僕チン and nonsense catchphrases. Give him a consistent babyish idiolect and one or two invented catchphrases reused every time.
- **First-Rate Guy** - ボカァ (an affected 僕は). Render as an affected drawl, e.g. "Yours truly". Smug, delivers one-word verdicts.
- **Geezer / Old Hag / Brew Hag** - ワシ / crone じゃ・のじゃ・ぞい. Crusty, archaic-rural English. Keep the three distinct: Geezer is sly, Old Hag is left-out and grumbling, Brew Hag is cheerful and businesslike.
- **Dark Dweller** - full chuunibyou. Darkness, flames, 貴様. Play it 100% straight; the joke is that nobody reacts.
- **Hot-Blooded Man** - shonen bellowing, 俺たち, big words in quote marks. All-caps sparingly, exclamation marks generously.
- **Sauna Sage / Tripping Sauna Guy / Sauna Guy / Sauna Guys A & B** - four distinct sauna registers: pseudo-profound theorist, spacey philosopher, screaming denier, terse gym-bro っす. Sauna Guys A & B speak in unison; keep their lines identical to each other.
- **Legendary Bathkeep** - working-class enforcer. Threats delivered as house rules, flat.
- **Mayor** - grovelling, drawn-out wails, always one register more desperate than the situation warrants.
- **Announcer** - stadium bark, all exclamation, no pronouns. Short lines only.
- **Pedestal** - katakana machine voice. Render as clipped ALL-CAPS machine English: "TRIAL ONE. COMMENCING."
- **Signboard / Notice / Graffiti / Statue / Stone Tablet / Room** - object speakers. Flat posted text, except 張り紙 (Notice), whose gag is that the sign abruptly starts *shouting* ("OCCUPIED" → "I SAID IT'S OCCUPIED!!"). Keep the tonal break.
- **Cow God / Moo-san / Cow** - moos only. Moo-san's moos carry menu prompts in parentheses: "Moo. (Return to town?)"

## 5. Honorifics: **KEEP**

Ruling: **keep Japanese honorific suffixes** (-sama, -san) romanized on the name.

Three reasons, in order of force:

1. The joke class-trainer NPCs are literally 【ファイターさん】/【プリーストさん】/【シーフさん】/【パイレーツさん】/【ウィッチさん】 - a polite suffix bolted onto a job noun. That gag is unrenderable without the suffix. Glossary: Fighter-san, Priest-san, Thief-san, Pirate-san, Mage-san.
2. 様 on スカーレット is near-universal and its *overuse* is itself a gag: the entire town, mid-collapse, still says スカーレット様. "Lady Scarlet" works in calm address and dies immediately at 「スカーレット様ァーーー！！」. So: **スカーレット様 → Scarlet-sama**, and screamed forms stretch the suffix ("Scarlet-samaaaaa!!").
3. The script runs a live address-register system underneath (貴女 / アンタ / てめぇ / 貴様 / お前 / 姉ちゃん / 主殿 / 殿方). Retaining the honorific tier keeps the rest of that ladder legible.

Non-suffix address terms are **translated**, not romanized:

| JP | EN |
|---|---|
| 主殿 | milady |
| 殿方 | gentleman (marks a female speaker) |
| お姉さん (address) | miss / hey, lady |
| 姉ちゃん (address) | sis / hey, sweetheart (leering) |
| 貴女 | you (cold, formal - keep the chill in the surrounding syntax) |

おじさん / お姉さん / 姉さん inside a **nameplate** are *not* "Uncle"/"Big Sister" - English does not call a stranger Uncle. See the glossary: they resolve to Guy / Man / Lady.

## 6. The 【NAME】 nameplate convention

Dialogue strings carry the speaker inside the string. The shape is:

```
【マスター】
「人が足りねぇ！　人が足りねぇよ！！」
```

Rules, all mandatory:

- Translate the name **using the `names` glossary**, nothing else. One English name per person, forever.
- **Keep 【 】 exactly.** Do not convert to [ ], < >, or a bare name. Do not add a colon.
- **Keep 「 」 exactly** around the body. Do not convert to " ". Inner quotes 『 』 also stay.
- **Keep the line break** between nameplate and body. One newline, in the same place.
- Do not move the name outside the brackets, do not prefix the body with it, do not merge the two lines.
- A nameplate not in the glossary means the extraction found a speaker nobody catalogued. **Transliterate it and flag the line** - do not invent an English name, because an invented name will not match the other 40 lines that speaker has.
- 【？？？】 stays **【???】**. It is a deliberate withheld-identity plate; never resolve it to the character's real name even when you know who it is.
- Combined plates (【街長＆街人】, 【女A＆B】, 【客紳士A＆B】, 【サウナーA＆B】) keep the ampersand form and must match their member rows exactly.

## 7. Control codes and sentinels

The extraction embeds engine tokens mid-string. **Every one is load-bearing.**

- `{W49}`, `{W47}`, `{W48}` - Wolf RPG wait/format sentinels. **Copy verbatim, same count, same relative position.** `{W49}` marks a beat where the JP put a full-width space; English keeps the token and puts its own sentence break at that point. Never delete one, never merge two, never add one.
- `\c[2]` ... `\c[0]` - color codes. Keep them hugging the same words they hugged in JP. If `\c[2]` opened before a skill name, it opens before the English skill name.
- `\i[126]` - inline icon. Stays at the same end of the string. Several reputation labels end in it.
- `【 】`, `「 」`, `～`, `…`, `♡`, `゛` on a vowel (お゛) - all meaningful. `♡` is a register marker; keep it.
- Full-width space (　) inside a line is a **beat**, not a word gap. Render as a sentence break, an em-space of punctuation, or a `{W49}`-adjacent pause - never as nothing.

If a string looks like editor-internal DB junk (`[ボス]…x1`, `弱1`, `※全体の適応値`, `ああああ`), it is a troop or config label, not dialogue. Translate per glossary and do not prettify.

## 8. Layout

- The message box is **~4 rows**, roughly **30-32 halfwidth cells** per row at the default font. This is hard.
- **Preserve the source's line-break structure.** One JP line = one EN line. If your English overruns, **cut words** - do not add a fifth line, do not reflow two lines into three.
- Nameplates: aim **≤16 cells**. That is why the glossary picks "Sea Dog" over "Man of the Sea" and "Awful Pickup Girl" over "Awful Pickup-Waiting Woman".
- Menu, status, and stat labels: **≤10 cells**, terse, no articles, no verbs. "Attack Up", not "Attack is increased".
- Status icons with one-kanji sources (封, 隠, 眠, 幻, ク+) get abbreviated English (Seal, Hidden, Sleep, Illus, Crit+). Their full-form siblings (封印 Sealed, 眠り Asleep) stay separate rows and separate strings.
- Battle log verbs stay terse and present-tense.

If those same kana appear inside a dialogue string, they are dialogue and they get translated.)

**The rule for everything not on the table.** First classify the sound:

1. **Creature or object noise** - invent an English spelling using English phonotactics. Keep the syllable count, keep the length: `ー` and `～` become repeated letters or an em dash, not a tilde. Keep the punctuation weight (`！！！！` stays four).
2. **Laugh** - build it from an English laugh consonant (h-, k-, g-) and keep the vowel pattern, so the *character's* laugh stays recognizably his across the script. One laugh spelling per character, fixed.
3. **Breath / body noise** - use English breath spellings (huff, hgk, wheeze) or an asterisked stage direction (`*hic*`), consistently within a character.
4. **Mimetic adverb (擬態語)** - these describe *manner*, not sound: じろじろ, そわそわ, もわわ～ん, めろめろ, ビリビリ. **Never transliterate these.** Translate them into an English verb or adverb. じろじろ is staring, not "jirojiro".

**Repetition is meaning.** ぎゅむぎゅむ is two beats; English gets two beats. 言え　言え　言え　言え is four; English gets four. Never compress a repeated chant into one word plus "repeatedly".

## 10. Pitfalls specific to this game

**Shop-role kanji are four different jobs.** 屋 = the business/its owner, 師 = the licensed practitioner, 主 = the proprietor, 長 = the manager, 番頭 = the head clerk. The glossary fixes them: マッサージ屋 Massage Boss vs マッサージ師 Masseur; 店主 Shopkeeper vs 店長 Manager; 伝説の番頭 Legendary Bathkeep. Do not swap them.

**Variant nameplates that are ONE speaker.** Same English both times, never numbered:
注意喚起おねえさん / 注意喚起お姉さん · ケツデカ姉さん / ケツデカお姉さん · 斜に構えた女 / 斜めに構えた女 · 力を欲する男 / 力が欲しい男 · ポジティブ / ポジティブな男 · セックス中の男 / セックスの中の男 (typo) · 恍惚お姉さん / 恍惚姉さん · 受付お姉さん / 受付のお姉さん · 処女チュウ / 秘・処女チュウ are the same creature but *different encounters*, so those two DO differ.

**Pairs that are genuinely different people - never merge:**
店主 / 店長 · 受付 / 受付お姉さん · 泥酔騎士 / 酔っ払い騎士 / 立ちんぼ騎士 · 街人 / 街民 / 町民 · 老婆 / ババア / 調合ババア · 触手 / エロ触手 · ユニコーン / S級闘技者ユニコーン · 限界職員 / 限界職員2 · 女A / 女B / 女A＆B · 恋する学生 / 恋する女学生 (same person, two scene states, two names by design) · ガキ / ガキ共 (singular vs. the pack).

**日向ぼっこGUY vs 日向ぼっこGAY** - a deliberate one-letter swap between the enemy and its troop name. Keep both, keep the caps, do not "fix" the typo.

**井戸王 (Well King)** exists as both an enemy and a 称号 title. Identical English is mandatory.

**極意 items unlock 称号 armor of the same name.** Chaos Hunter Secrets → Chaos Hunter. Sea Saver Secrets → Sea Saver. Money Dragon Secrets → Money Dragon. Crimson Knight Secrets → Crimson Knight. The stems must match or the unlock reads as unrelated.

**The ～ナール drug family** (イキタクナール, エロクナール, ムラツクール, チンコナオース...) is a coined fake-pharma set. English uses a "-nol" suffix family. Never translate one descriptively - the joke is that they all sound like the same brand.

**The baseball cluster** (一番打者 Leadoff Batter, 三冠王 Triple Crown, スラッガー Slugger, 番長 Gang Boss, 期待のルーキー Promising Rookie) is a deliberate running metaphor. Keep the baseball vocabulary; do not neutralize it into generic praise.

**Self-reference jokes.** ボカァ, 僕チン, ワシ, あたくし, 自分, 俺様, 私め - each is a character's whole gag. Give each a distinctive English idiolect and never let two characters share one.

**"Legendary" things that are not.** 伝説の薬草 (enemy, Legendary Herb) vs 伝説の薬草？ (item, Legendary Herb?) - the question mark is part of the item name and is the punchline. Keep it.

**チェスト** is the Jigen-ryu swordsman's battle cry ("Chesto!"), not the noun "chest".
**マグロ** is a tuna *and* the slang for a motionless partner; the skill マグロタイム takes the English idiom (Dead Fish Time) while the fish item stays Tuna.
**見抜き** is a coined shop-service word with no English equivalent; the glossary fixes it as Peepshow.
**中1** is a school-year label. Translate it as a school year and put it in no other kind of sentence.

**Register words that are NOT synonyms:** ビッチ / ヤリマン / 肉便器 / セフレ / ウリ sit at different points on the degradation scale and the game uses the difference mechanically (they are equippable titles that change NPC reactions). Keep five distinct English words: Bitch / Slut / Cumdump / Fuckbuddy / Hooker.

**エロ vs ドスケベ vs スケベ** is also a scale: Lewd < Perv < Filthy. Keep them ordered.

**Duplicate DB rows are normal in Wolf RPG.** The same JP string can appear as an enemy, a troop, and an equipment title. When it does, all instances take the identical English string - the glossary is the single source of truth, and consistency beats per-context elegance every time.
