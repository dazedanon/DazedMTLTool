- **There is no regional dialect anywhere in this game.** A sweep of the whole corpus finds zero やねん,
  あかん, せやな, じゃけん, だべ, ずら or ござる. Every voice in the game is standard Japanese separated purely
  by **register** - polite versus plain, 私 versus 俺, 貴女 versus アンタ versus 貴様 versus お前 versus 君.
  So do not invent an accent. A rough speaker gets rough *diction*, never dropped g's, "ain't", Cockney,
  Southern or Yorkshire. The only pseudo-archaic voices are deliberate costumes (see the ninja and the
  hillbilly-elder bullets below), and they are jokes, not geography.

- **Five second-person pronouns are in live contrast and the game uses the contrast for meaning.**
  貴女 (cold formality, Sofia and Sensei to Artesia) / アンタ (Artesia's default, familiar and rude) /
  貴様 (contempt or martial hostility) / お前・てめぇ (aggression) / 君 (a superior being pleasant to an
  inferior). They are not interchangeable and they should not all become a flat "you" when the sentence can
  carry the difference through word choice, contraction or address term.

- **Honorific habits that are not the standard set.** Keep -san / -chan / -sama / -kun as usual, and on top
  of that: `執聖官様` is a *rank* plus 様 and becomes "Lady Holy Executor" or "Your Excellency", never
  "Holy-Executor-sama". `アルテシア先生` (from the slum jailer and the inmates, to a woman hired to run a
  masturbation class) keeps its straight-faced "Teacher Artesia". `義父殿` from the fiancé is deliberately
  over-formal against an otherwise rough voice. `お主` belongs only to the elderly Director. `バイトちゃん`
  ("part-timer-chan") is what the PR department calls Artesia instead of her name. `お姉さん` and `姉ちゃん`
  from children and drunks are **not** honorifics - they are "lady", "miss" or "sis" as the scene needs.

- **Item and skill descriptions are a fixed two-part shape: a mechanical line, then a deadpan punchline.**
  `ヨケヨケブーツ` = "素早さと回避率が上がる。/ 当たらなければどうということはない。" (a Gundam quotation).
  `ゴールデンパンチ` = "相手は死ぬ / 消費MP1". `覚醒ドリンク` = "嘘である。ただの水。特に効果はない".
  `オナ禁書目録` = "もうオナ禁なんてしないなんて言わないよ絶対。". Keep both halves, keep the joke line
  **short**, and keep it dry - the humour is in the flatness, so never expand it into a sentence that
  explains itself. A trailing `消費MP50` line is a fixed cost tag: render it identically everywhere
  ("MP Cost: 50"), never fold it into the prose.

- **The eight sin-zodiac accessories are ONE story told in eight fragments and must be translated as a
  connected sequence.** `永劫をなげうってでも、彼女のすべてが欲しかった` (強欲のトォリス) →
  `永劫により止まった時間が彼女との蜜月で溶けていった` (色欲のアクエリアス) →
  `君を奴らになど渡したくなかった。君に永劫は似合わない` (嫉妬のヴィルゴ) →
  `気づくべきであった。彼女を失ってしまうことに` (怠惰のパイシーズ) →
  `守り切れるはずだった。なぜ彼女は死んだ？` (傲慢のリオン) →
  `永劫を共にした者たちから向けられたのは祝福ではなく憤怒であった` (憤怒のカプリコーン) →
  `永劫などでは最初から満たされるはずもなかった` (暴食のカンケル) →
  `同胞の永劫をを捨て、彼女との未来を選んだ。それが私の罪` (原罪のサジタリウス). One first-person immortal
  speaking about one mortal woman. **永劫 must be the same English word in all eight** ("eternity"), the
  register must stay elevated and elegiac, and the fragments must read as a chain when a player lines them up
  in the inventory. Translated one at a time in isolation, this motif dies.

- **`〜おじさん` is a naming convention, not a description, and there are about thirty of them.**
  `体験版おじさん`, `口笛おじさん`, `おさんぽおじさん`, `人間観察おじさん`, `早撃ちおじさん`,
  `肩までつかるおじさん`, `考えるおじさん`, `無垢おじさん`, `気品のあるおじさん`, `達観おじさん`,
  `汗かきおじさん`, `全開放おじさん`, `水着配りおじさん`, `スケベポイント交換おじさん`, `回想部屋おじさん`,
  `おっぱいを解放しろおじさん`. Each is a man defined by exactly one behaviour. Use a single fixed English
  frame - "the Whistling Guy", "the Strolling Guy", "the People-Watching Guy" - so the family reads as a
  family. Do not translate おじさん as "uncle", and do not let one of them become "Mister" while the rest are
  "Guy". The same convention runs through `〜隊員` (a dozen Peacekeeping corpsmen each named for one vice),
  `〜博徒` (five gamblers), `〜ニンジャ`, `〜水夫` and `〜ババア`.

- **`元〜` is a running redemption gag.** `元犬` (a man who was turned into a dog), `元乞食`, `元勇者`,
  `元雪だるま`, `元・男の娘`, `元金持ち`, `元荒ぶる婚活女`, `元悪のマッサージ師`, `元EDおじさん`. The joke is
  always that a nameplate updates the instant the character's arc resolves. Render the prefix with a single
  consistent word ("Ex-" or "Former", pick one and hold it) so the change is visible on screen.

- **`アルテシアの名声が轟いた！` is a fixed reward banner** ("Artesia's fame thundered out!") and it fires
  dozens of times with a different prize each time. One English wording, every occurrence. The `威信` twin
  `アルテシアの威信と名声が1上がった！` behaves the same way.

- **`オナ禁` (abstaining from masturbation) is a plot-level running gag**, not a one-off. It is a clue in the
  Chapter 5 investigation (`『協力者』はオナ禁していたようだ。目的もないのにオナ禁なんてしない`), an accessory
  (`オナ禁書目録`), a monster's motivation (`オナ禁中なんだぞこっちは！`) and a piece of moral philosophy
  (`悪法もまた法なり……オナ禁もまたオナニー……っと`). Use one English term for it across all of them, and keep it
  blunt - the joke only works if the same phrase recurs.

- **`スケベ` as a prefix is the game's own enemy-naming system.** `スケベゴブリン`, `スケベミミック`,
  `スケベ術師`, `スケベ盗賊`, `スケベ触手`, `スケベマルノミスネーク`, plus the graded arena fighters
  `S級スケベ闘士` through `H級スケベ闘士`. One fixed English adjective for every one of them, and keep the
  rank letters (S/A/B/C/H) exactly as written - the H grade is the punchline.

- **The fake-ninja tic `〜でニンジャ！`.** `デカニンジャ` ends every sentence with it as a pun on 〜でござる,
  and mixes in 拙者, お主 and 候 (`拙者の知的好奇心を満たして候！`). Build one absurd pseudo-archaic English tag
  and use it on every single line of his, because the tic *is* the character. `カクレニンジャ`, `早漏ニンジャ`
  and `ニンジャ` are separate ninja and do not share it.

- **Made-up words built for sound rather than sense.** The skills `灰暴瑠手慧慈` and `恕弩恕道` (spelled
  `怒弩恕道` on its Condition entry) are biker-gang graffiti *ateji* - the kanji are chosen phonetically and
  mean nothing when read literally, so do not gloss them character by character. The speaker names
  `倍返シスト` and `腹下シスト` are the same trick with a fake `-ist` suffix welded onto a Japanese phrase.
  In both cases produce an English name with the same "deliberately misspelt tough-guy" energy and lock one
  spelling, including across the ateji variants.

- **Vocalisations are transliterated as English sounds, never left in kana and never romanised.**
  あっ / んっ / はぁ become "Ah-", "Nngh-", "Haah-". `イく` / `イっちゃう` are "I'm cumming" / "I'm gonna cum".
  `しゅるるるるるるる` (the tentacles and the anemone, over a thousand lines) is one locked English slither.
  `みみっくぅうう～～～` is one locked Mimic cry. A dictionary word stays a word however many vowels are
  stretched onto it: `いやぁあああ` is "Nooooooo!" and never "Iyaaaaa!", and the same goes for やめて, だめ,
  すごい, きもちいい.

- **Artesia's speech physically degrades across an H scene and the degradation is the content.** Her normal
  sharp voice slurs stage by stage: `気持ちいい` → `きもひいい`, `イク` → `イグ`, `わたし` → `わらひ`,
  `だから` → `らったら`, and `お゛っ` / `あ゛ーっ` / `ふぐぅおぉおお゛` appear once she has lost the shape of
  her own mouth. **The dakuten ゛ stays and the kana does not**: `お゛っ` becomes something like `Ogh゛-`,
  never `Oッ`. Escalate the English spelling in step with the Japanese instead of writing every line in clean
  prose, and never tidy a slurred line back into grammar.

- **`ふーっ！♡　ふーっ！♡` is heavy breathing, not a sigh of relief**, and it recurs over four thousand times as a
  scene-pacing beat on its own row. Render it as panting of comparable length ("Haah-! Haah-!") and keep it
  as its own line. Standalone SFX rows are pacing - never expand one into a sentence.

- **`{0}` in the battle and shop strings is a name the engine substitutes, so English needs the grammar built
  around it.** `{0}の攻撃！` → "{0}'s attack!", `{0}は{1}を発動した！` → "{0} used {1}!",
  `しかし{0}が足りなかった！` → "But there wasn't enough {0}!". Third person, present or simple past, and the
  placeholder must survive - these are shown for whichever actor is acting, and one of them is Artesia.

- **Signboards, notices and articles are read text, not speech.** `看板`, `案内立札`, `立札`, `注意看板`,
  `張り紙`, `記事①②③`, `アナウンス`, `おしらせ`, and objects used as speakers (`宝箱`, `扉`, `風車`,
  `教材`). Render them as signage: terse, impersonal, no speech contractions, no quotation marks.

- **Parody quotations are lifted from other media and should land as recognisable English quotations, not as
  literal glosses.** `当たらなければどうということはない` (Gundam), `相手は死ぬ`,
  `もうオナ禁なんてしないなんて言わないよ絶対` (a pop-song lyric), `悪法もまた法なり` (Socrates),
  `ここから先は『押忍』の世界……` (`押忍のハチマキ`). If an English equivalent quotation exists, use it. If not,
  write a line that reads as a quotation of something, and never footnote the reference.
